3.1. Annual and Peak Electricity Demands
Future electricity demand is projected to grow due to new large loads and electrification 
of transportation and buildings. In all pathways scenarios, new large loads, such as 
manufacturing and data center projects interconnecting to the system, drive growing 
electricity demand across both annual loads and peaks (Figure 14).43 Planning early for 
abundant supply for these large industrial and commercial projects can ensure continued 
opportunities for economic growth.

5.1.6. MANAGE THE PACE OF COMBUSTION UNIT RETIREMENTS TO 
MAINTAIN RELIABILITY
New Y ork’s generation fleet is aging, the rate of renewable deployment is uncertain, reliability 
margins are shrinking, and significant levels of large loads are being added. Due to these 
factors, the State will need to be strategic about the pace of combustion unit retirements 
and/or replacements as it works toward its clean energy goals and to meet reliability needs 
as quickly and cost-effectively as possible. Combustion-generating units will remain essential 
parts of electric grid reliability and affordability, and the retirement of these units will not 
be able to occur until resources that provide the same grid reliability attributes are put in

5.4.1. MEET THE GROWING ENERGY NEEDS OF INDUSTRY AND OTHER LARGE 
ENERGY USERS
Energy demand from industry is expected to grow, especially from new high-tech and 
energy-intensive industries. Continued State coordination and investments in reliable, clean, 
and affordable power are needed. 
Data centers are projected to be a major driver of new industrial load. Planning 
considerations for large data centers are complex, as uncertainty exists around their location, 
long-term energy consumption, and demand flexibility. In general, expanding the electric 
customer base should bring in new utility revenue and spread system costs over a larger rate 
base. But the size of new large industrial users has raised concerns of ratepayers subsidizing 
these industries. New Y ork’s interconnection process for new large loads is designed to fairly 
allocate energy system build-out or upgrade costs to the user that necessitates such costs, 
consistent with “beneficiary pays” cost-causation principles.56 The State will continue to work 
to align electricity market structures with the emerging dynamics of large loads and broader 
policy goals.
Recommendations
• Continue to advance interagency coordination and planning on generation and 
transmission infrastructure investments.
• Continue to pursue new rate designs and demand-side programs that provide 
competitive utility rates, reduce the cost of industrial electrification, and incentivize 
reduced or flexible usage of energy. Consider rate designs that differentiate charges 
based on peak and off-peak usage; incentivize flexible usage, including on-site 
energy generation; and value the storage and/or recycling of thermal energy.
