Only Dominion Energy Virginia and Virginia put hard cost terms on data centers; the other plans stop at principle or mention

Large-load policy matrix across 11 public state energy plans and utility resource plans · built 2026-11-23

Where this started: the state energy-plan RAG assistant from my Research Analyst internship at the Hamm Institute for American Energy at Oklahoma State University (2025), extended from answering questions to comparing policies.

Executive summary

Situation. Data centers are the main driver of new US electricity demand, and regulators are deciding who pays for the grid they need. State plans and utility resource plans are where that stance is written down, but they are long and inconsistent, so they are hard to compare.

Complication. Reading them by hand does not scale, and a language model summary is not trustworthy unless every claim can be traced to the page it came from.

Resolution. This study codes 11 of 11 documents against 8 attributes. Every claim carries a quote that an automated guard confirmed appears word for word on the cited page. The result: only Dominion Energy Virginia and Virginia score 100 on ratepayer protection, meaning each has a dedicated rate class, a financial commitment and a cost-allocation principle on the page. New York and New Jersey score 33 each, resting on a cost-allocation principle alone, and Maine, Oregon and Utah score 0 on the pages retrieved.

So what. For a planner or analyst, this is the template to compare other states against, and the gap to ask about elsewhere.

5. Limits you should know

  • Coder. The status labels and quotes were written by an AI assistant reading the excerpts in one session. The repository also ships a Groq-based extractor (largeload/extract.py) for a fully automated run; it was not run for this report.
  • Excerpts, not full pages. The guard checks quotes against text windows around large-load terms, not whole pages. The retrieval in the browser step is close to, but not identical with, the Python retrieval in the repo.
  • Provenance. The three New York volumes were supplied as PDF files and excerpted with the repo's Python retrieval; the other eight were excerpted in a browser with pdf.js and hash-checked after transcription. Text layers differ (the New York PDF spells “New York” as “New Y ork”, and one quote keeps that spelling so it matches the page).
  • Coverage. Maine and Utah returned almost no large-load text, so their zeros mean “nothing retrieved”. One PacifiCorp excerpt was cut off before the relevant sentence, so that cell is left empty.
  • Meaning. The matrix records what a document says, not what is law or tariff in force. Utility plans and state plans are different kinds of document and are shown as separate rows.
  • Sample. 11 documents is a demonstration, not a survey of US policy.
