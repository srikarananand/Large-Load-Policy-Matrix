manage rapid load growth with a more distributed, flexible, and lower-impact resource mix: a statewide 10 GW DER
target, 10% data center demand flexibility during peak periods,

Data center demand flexibility: Data-center demand flexibility is the ability of large
customers to reduce, shift, or manage electricity use when the grid is most constrained. It can
be delivered on site through load shifting, on-site storage, or curtailing operations, or through
verified off-site resources and contracts—including VPPs, distributed solar and storage,
and DR—that reduce system peak elsewhere on the grid. Reducing load during the highest-
demand hours lowers the amount of new capacity and grid infrastructure the system must
build, while improving utilization of existing infrastructure. The Distributed Flexibility Scenario
assumes all data centers provide the equivalent of a 10% reduction in peak demand.

Ensure data center growth advances Virginia’s energy goals
Context: Virginia is the world’s largest data-center market, and VEP modeling projects that data centers could account
for more than 90% of the Commonwealth’s electricity-demand growth over the next two decades. Rapid data center
load growth is driving grid infrastructure constraints and higher costs, including at the PJM wholesale-market level, as
discussed in Section 2G: Energy Affordability. Continuing to implement robust protections and cost allocation practices
will be essential to protect residential, small businesses, and other ratepayers from the infrastructure costs caused by
data centers.
