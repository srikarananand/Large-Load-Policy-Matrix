2.6.5. Affordability Considerations  
New York State acknowledges that there is a growing energy affordability crisis—bills are increasing, and 
many households are struggling to pay their monthly utility bills.  
While New York’s grid continues to serve New Yorkers with reliable power, key structural trends require 
continued attention and a strategic approach: the aging of the existing generation fleet and utility 
infrastructure; the rapid growth of demand, including large loads and electrification targets; and 
challenges in developing new dispatchable resources. New York has a significant amount of utility 
infrastructure that is rapidly approaching, or at the end of, its useful life. The thousands of miles of aging 
utility poles, substations and other equipment, plus investments to develop new and maintain existing 
generation resources (of which our existing generation is among the oldest in the country), will require 
spending billions of dollars to ensure system reliability and increase system resiliency. For example,

2.10.8. Interconnection 
The NYISO interconnection process ensures open access to the transmission grid for new generators, 
transmission projects, and certain large loads seeking to connect. Most proposed new projects must 
adhere to the NYISO interconnection process.56 The process determines, among other things, what 
system upgrades are needed to permit new projects to connect to the transmission system and to 
ensure deliverability of their energy output.

• The State will need to be strategic about the pace of combustion unit retirements and/or 
replacements as it works to pursue achievement of its clean energy targets. New York’s 
generation fleet is aging, the rate of renewable deployment is uncertain, reliability margins are 
shrinking, and significant levels of large loads are being added. Due to these factors, the State 
will need to be strategic about the pace of combustion unit retirements and/or replacements as 
it works towards its clean energy goals and to meet reliability needs as quickly and cost-
effectively as possible. Combustion generating units will remain essential parts of electric grid 
reliability and affordability. Retirement of these units will not be able to occur until resources 
that provide the same grid reliability attributes are put in place.
